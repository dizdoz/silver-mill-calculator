The ring size describes the INSIDE of the finished ring. Silver thickness therefore matters: the strip length follows the centre line, so approximately π × thickness is added to the internal circumference. Your join/sizing allowance is then added on top. This is a starting calculation; your exact forming method may require a different allowance.

The maths

Square size: (desired width + half desired thickness) ÷ 1.5.

Finished weight: thickness × width × length × 10.36 ÷ 1,000.

Starting melt: finished weight × (1 + loss allowance), rounded up to the nearest 0.5 g.

Circumference: diameter × π.

Ring size: centre-line circumference = internal circumference + (π × silver thickness), then the join/sizing allowance is added.

Density used: 10.36 g/cm³ for sterling 925. The 30% loss allowance is deliberately conservative for student use and can be changed.
